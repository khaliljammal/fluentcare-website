A few well-chosen Spanish phrases help your front desk greet patients, check them in, book the next visit and explain what they owe. This guide groups short, polite phrases by front-desk moment, with a simple pronunciation guide and a printable cheat sheet at the end.

The phrases use neutral Latin American Spanish and the formal *usted*, which is the safe default with adult patients. They cover courtesy and logistics. A phrase sheet doesn't make anyone an interpreter, so check the [interpreter section](#when-to-use-a-qualified-interpreter) before you rely on it.

## Greeting and check-in

Start warmly, then confirm the basics. Asking which language a person prefers early saves everyone time later.

| English | Spanish | Say it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Good morning. / Good afternoon. | Buenos días. / Buenas tardes. | BWEH-nohs DEE-ahs / BWEH-nahs TAR-dehs |
| I speak a little Spanish. | Hablo un poco de español. | AH-bloh oon POH-koh deh ehs-pah-NYOHL |
| Do you have an appointment? | ¿Tiene cita? | TYEH-neh SEE-tah |
| Is this your first visit? | ¿Es su primera visita? | ehs soo pree-MEH-rah vee-SEE-tah |
| What is your name? | ¿Cuál es su nombre? | KWAHL ehs soo NOHM-breh |
| What is your date of birth? | ¿Cuál es su fecha de nacimiento? | KWAHL ehs soo FEH-chah deh nah-see-MYEHN-toh |
| Which language do you prefer to speak? | ¿En qué idioma prefiere hablar? | ehn KEH ee-DYOH-mah preh-FYEH-reh ah-BLAR |
| We can offer you an interpreter at no cost. | Le podemos ofrecer un intérprete sin costo. | leh poh-DEH-mohs oh-freh-SEHR oon een-TEHR-preh-teh seen KOHS-toh |
| Please have a seat. | Por favor, tome asiento. | por fah-VOR, TOH-meh ah-SYEHN-toh |

Many Spanish speakers have two surnames: the father's first, then the mother's. For María López García, *López* is usually the main surname. Ask how the person prefers their name recorded, and keep it consistent across insurance, forms and reminders.

## Insurance and forms

Keep each request to one item. Point to the card or the line on the form as you speak.

| English | Spanish | Say it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do you have dental insurance? | ¿Tiene seguro dental? | TYEH-neh seh-GOO-roh dehn-TAHL |
| May I see your insurance card and a photo ID? | ¿Me permite su tarjeta del seguro y una identificación con foto? | meh pehr-MEE-teh soo tar-HEH-tah dehl seh-GOO-roh ee OO-nah ee-dehn-tee-fee-kah-SYOHN kohn FOH-toh |
| Please fill out this form. | Por favor, llene este formulario. | por fah-VOR, YEH-neh EHS-teh for-moo-LAH-ryoh |
| Please sign here. | Firme aquí, por favor. | FEER-meh ah-KEE, por fah-VOR |
| Has your address or phone number changed? | ¿Ha cambiado su dirección o su número de teléfono? | ah kahm-BYAH-doh soo dee-rehk-SYOHN oh soo NOO-meh-roh deh teh-LEH-foh-noh |
| The dental team will review your health history with you. | El equipo dental revisará su historial médico con usted. | ehl eh-KEE-poh dehn-TAHL reh-vee-sah-RAH soo ees-toh-RYAHL MEH-dee-koh kohn oos-TEHD |

Hand over forms that have already been translated by a qualified translator where you have them. Health history questions belong with the clinical team, with language support arranged.

## Scheduling and reminders

Dates and times are where small mistakes cost the most. Say the day of the week, the date and the time, then ask the patient to repeat it back.

| English | Spanish | Say it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What day would you like to come in? | ¿Qué día le gustaría venir? | KEH DEE-ah leh goos-tah-REE-ah veh-NEER |
| Is morning or afternoon better for you? | ¿Le queda mejor en la mañana o en la tarde? | leh KEH-dah meh-HOR ehn lah mah-NYAH-nah oh ehn lah TAR-deh |
| Your next appointment is Tuesday, March 3, at 10 a.m. | Su próxima cita es el martes 3 de marzo a las 10 de la mañana. | soo PROHK-see-mah SEE-tah ehs ehl MAR-tehs TREHS deh MAR-soh ah lahs DYEHS deh lah mah-NYAH-nah |
| Can you tell me the date and time of your appointment? | ¿Me puede decir la fecha y la hora de su cita? | meh PWEH-deh deh-SEER lah FEH-chah ee lah OH-rah deh soo SEE-tah |
| Please arrive 15 minutes before your appointment. | Por favor, llegue 15 minutos antes de su cita. | por fah-VOR, YEH-geh KEEN-seh mee-NOO-tohs AHN-tehs deh soo SEE-tah |
| We will send you a text message reminder. | Le enviaremos un recordatorio por mensaje de texto. | leh ehn-vyah-REH-mohs oon reh-kor-dah-TOH-ryoh por mehn-SAH-heh deh TEHKS-toh |
| If you need to cancel or change, please call us 24 hours ahead. | Si necesita cancelar o cambiar su cita, llámenos con 24 horas de anticipación. | see neh-seh-SEE-tah kahn-seh-LAR oh kahm-BYAR soo SEE-tah, YAH-meh-nohs kohn VEYN-tee-KWAH-troh OH-rahs deh ahn-tee-see-pah-SYOHN |

Write the date on the appointment card as well, with the month spelled out: *martes, 3 de marzo*. Many Spanish speakers read 3/4 as 3 April, not March 4.

## Costs and payment

Money questions are common and stressful. Use the actual amount, show it on paper or screen, and swap in your own payment options.

| English | Spanish | Say it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your copay today is $25. | Su copago de hoy es de 25 dólares. | soo koh-PAH-goh deh OY ehs deh VEYN-tee-SEEN-koh DOH-lah-rehs |
| Your insurance covers part of the cost. | Su seguro cubre una parte del costo. | soo seh-GOO-roh KOO-breh OO-nah PAR-teh dehl KOHS-toh |
| Here is your treatment estimate. | Aquí está el presupuesto de su tratamiento. | ah-KEE ehs-TAH ehl preh-soo-PWEHS-toh deh soo trah-tah-MYEHN-toh |
| How would you like to pay? | ¿Cómo desea pagar? | KOH-moh deh-SEH-ah pah-GAR |
| We accept cash, cards and payment plans. | Aceptamos efectivo, tarjeta y planes de pago. | ah-sehp-TAH-mohs eh-fehk-TEE-voh, tar-HEH-tah ee PLAH-nehs deh PAH-goh |
| Here is your receipt. | Aquí tiene su recibo. | ah-KEE TYEH-neh soo reh-SEE-boh |

Handing over an estimate is routine. Explaining what a treatment involves, or asking someone to sign a payment agreement, needs fuller language support.

## After the visit

Keep checkout to logistics: the next visit, the paperwork and who to call. Aftercare and medication instructions come from the clinical team, in language the patient understands.

| English | Spanish | Say it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Here are your written instructions. | Aquí tiene sus instrucciones por escrito. | ah-KEE TYEH-neh soos eens-trook-SYOH-nehs por ehs-KREE-toh |
| Do you have any questions? | ¿Tiene alguna pregunta? | TYEH-neh ahl-GOO-nah preh-GOON-tah |
| Your next cleaning is in six months. | Su próxima limpieza es en seis meses. | soo PROHK-see-mah leem-PYEH-sah ehs ehn SEYS MEH-sehs |
| If you have questions after your visit, call this number. | Si tiene preguntas después de su visita, llame a este número. | see TYEH-neh preh-GOON-tahs dehs-PWEHS deh soo vee-SEE-tah, YAH-meh ah EHS-teh NOO-meh-roh |
| Have a nice day. | Que tenga un buen día. | keh TEHN-gah oon BWEHN DEE-ah |

If the patient has a question about pain, bleeding, medication or their treatment, don't answer it from a phrase sheet. Bring in the clinical team and arrange language support.

## On the phone

Phone calls are harder than face-to-face: no gestures, no paper to point at. Slow down, confirm details twice and transfer early.

| English | Spanish | Say it |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Could you speak more slowly, please? | ¿Puede hablar más despacio, por favor? | PWEH-deh ah-BLAR MAHS dehs-PAH-syoh, por fah-VOR |

| Can you spell your last name? | ¿Me puede deletrear su apellido? | meh PWEH-deh deh-leh-treh-AR soo ah-peh-YEE-doh |
| What is your phone number? | ¿Cuál es su número de teléfono? | KWAHL ehs soo NOO-meh-roh deh teh-LEH-foh-noh |
| Please hold. | No cuelgue, por favor. | noh KWEHL-geh, por fah-VOR |

## Pronunciation tips and common mistakes

Spanish is spelled the way it sounds, so a few rules cover most of these phrases.

- **Vowels are short and pure:** a = *ah*, e = *eh*, i = *ee*, o = *oh*, u = *oo*. Don't glide them.
- **Stress:** an accent mark shows the stressed syllable (*teléfono*). Without one, words ending in a vowel, *n* or *s* stress the second-to-last syllable (*cita*, *tarjeta*).
- **ll and y** sound like the English *y* in most of Latin America: *llene* is YEH-neh.
- **ñ** sounds like *ny* in canyon: *mañana*.
- **j**, and **g** before e or i, sound like a breathy *h*: *tarjeta* is tar-HEH-tah.
- **h** is silent: *hora* is OH-rah.
- **z**, and **c** before e or i, sound like *s* in Latin America: *nacimiento*.

A few mistakes come up again and again at the front desk:

- **Switching to *tú*.** Use *usted* with adult patients unless they invite you to be less formal.
- **Writing dates in US order only.** Spell out the month so 3/4 can't be misread.
- **Asking "¿Me entiende?"** ("Do you understand me?"). Most people say yes. Ask them to repeat the key detail back instead.
- **Mixing up *cita* and *fecha*.** *Cita* is the appointment; *fecha* is the calendar date.
- **Falling for *embarazada*.** It means pregnant, not embarrassed. If a patient says it, tell the clinical team.
- **Nodding along.** If you didn't catch something, say *¿Me lo puede repetir, por favor?* It's always better than guessing.

## When to use a qualified interpreter

These phrases are for courtesy and logistics. They don't make you a qualified interpreter or qualified bilingual staff. Bring in a qualified interpreter for:

- Explaining a diagnosis, treatment plan, risks or alternatives.
- Informed consent and anything else the patient signs about their care.
- Health history, medications, allergies, pain or symptoms.
- Aftercare and medication instructions.
- Complaints, billing disputes and payment agreements.
- Any time you're unsure the patient understood, or the patient asks for one.

Don't ask children to interpret, and don't rely on a relative or friend as a substitute for a qualified interpreter. If your practice is covered by Section 1557 of the Affordable Care Act, for example because it receives federal financial assistance such as Medicaid payments, [45 CFR 92.201](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-92/subpart-C/section-92.201) requires free, accurate and timely language assistance and limits when companions or minors may interpret. Check your obligations with your own advisers. AHRQ's [Address Language Differences tool](https://www.ahrq.gov/health-literacy/improve/precautions/tool9.html) is a practical starting point for your clinic's process.

Agree your interpreter route before you need it: who to call, how to connect, and who tells the dentist. Our [language-access workflow guide](/resources/language-barriers-in-healthcare/) walks through the setup.

## Frequently asked questions

### What Spanish should a dental front desk learn first?

Start with greetings, the appointment and date-of-birth questions, insurance, signing forms and the appointment date and time. Those cover most check-ins. Add "¿En qué idioma prefiere hablar?" so you can arrange the right support early.

### Should I use *tú* or *usted* with patients?

Use *usted* with adult patients. It's polite and expected in a healthcare setting. Switch only if the patient invites you to.

### Is this phrase guide enough to talk to Spanish-speaking patients?

It's enough for courtesy and simple logistics. It isn't enough for treatment, consent, health history or aftercare. For those, use a qualified interpreter.

### Can a family member interpret instead?

Don't rely on one as a substitute for a qualified interpreter, and never ask a child. If your practice is covered by Section 1557, the rule limits when an accompanying adult or minor may interpret.

### Can I use a translation app at the front desk?

For routine conversations, a translation app can help alongside your interpreter process. Check how it handles audio and data, and keep a qualified interpreter available. Our guide to [evaluating translation apps for healthcare](/resources/translation-apps-for-healthcare/) has a checklist.
