When evaluating translation apps for healthcare, start with the conversation your team needs to handle. Compare the workflow, staff access, data processing, current availability and cost. Keep your clinic’s interpreter process alongside that evaluation. A feature list or a fluent demonstration does not establish clinical suitability.

## Start with one clinic task

Imagine a practice manager and receptionist comparing tools for an invented appointment-scheduling conversation. They use no patient names, records or health details. This fictional exercise gives every option the same task, device and questions.

Write down what you need: spoken replies in both directions, a way to pause, individual staff access, or a shared clinic allowance. Separately record questions that need documentary evidence, such as provider agreements and evaluated language support. A successful demonstration cannot answer those questions.

## Compare the kind of support

Use this table to keep different approaches separate. It is a purchasing checklist, not a vendor ranking or a finding that a category is suitable for patient use.

| Option you are considering | What to ask or demonstrate | What still needs evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two-way voice translation | Can colleagues take turns, hear both replies and pause? | Intended use, evaluated language pairs, audio processing and applicable agreements |
| A fixed-phrase tool | Does its actual phrase library cover your task and language? | How phrases were reviewed, current coverage and intended limits |
| Written or document translation | Is the task a written document or a spoken exchange? | Review process, document handling and suitability for that purpose |
| Qualified interpreter support | How does your team reach an interpreter when needed? | Your service arrangements, availability and escalation process |

Ask each provider the same questions. Record a source and date for every answer. If a capability is unclear, leave it unresolved rather than awarding a checkmark. Our [language-access workflow guide](/resources/language-barriers-in-healthcare/) helps organise the surrounding clinic process.

## Check the practical buying details

Before choosing an app, collect answers in six areas:

- **Staff access:** Who creates the clinic account, invites staff and removes access?
- **Language and device:** Is your intended language pair available in the current release, and what evaluation evidence exists for it?
- **Connection and controls:** What happens when the network drops, someone needs to pause or the audio is unclear?
- **Data handling:** Who processes audio, what does the application store, and what do provider settings and agreements cover?
- **Price and usage:** Is the allowance per person or per clinic? What happens when it runs out, and who controls additional spending?
- **Availability and support:** Can your team access the actual release, and where do questions go?

Request current policy and pricing documents. Distinguish a published promise from something you have verified in your own evaluation. Do not assume that a configured language or an advertised feature has been clinically validated.

Owners manage team access and the subscription; staff sign in individually and share the clinic’s minutes. FluentCare does not connect you to human interpreters.

AI translation can make mistakes. Use a qualified interpreter for informed consent, complex or high-stakes decisions, unresolved uncertainty, or whenever one is requested or required.

## Finish with a recorded decision

Keep the fictional task, observed control or audio problems, source links and unanswered buying questions in one evaluation record. A staff exercise can reveal usability problems; it does not produce an accuracy score or prove readiness for patient use.
